# Getting Started with Xenofactions

This guide explains the basic gameplay loop for a new player on a Xenofactions server.

Open the faction menu (K by default) for **Start here** and **Help**. These pages keep their selected page when revisited. Buttons use Tab/Shift+Tab or arrow keys for selection and Enter/Space for activation; mouse clicks, the wheel and on-screen scroll arrows are also available. Descriptions stay below their actions. Action forms support Tab/Shift+Tab and Enter; Escape leaves a setup form for normal play.

The welcome introduction shows **Open world map: <current key>** and **Open faction menu: <current key>** below its heading. These use XenoFactions' own bindings in Minecraft Controls, including mouse buttons and **Unbound** for an unassigned action. The new **Open Xenofactions World Map** binding starts unbound; assign it in Controls or open World map from the faction menu. It is separate from other mods' map bindings. Reopening the welcome after changing Controls shows the new assignments; both hints stay above the paginated introduction.

Automatic welcome display is an installation-wide client preference stored in the exact file `tutorialflag` in Minecraft's game directory (`mcDataDir`). Its presence suppresses the automatic welcome. Its absence queues the welcome once per connection, after login synchronization and when existing screen/setup priorities allow. Dismissing the introduction (including Escape), **Start here**, and **Explore map** create the marker through the same completion path. Delete `tutorialflag` and reconnect to see the welcome again, or confirm **Replay welcome** in Help to delete it and queue the welcome in the current connection. File errors are logged without crashing. **Modpack exports must omit `tutorialflag` so new installations see the welcome.**

Old server-side `OnboardingComplete` values are ignored and left untouched; they never recreate the local marker. Welcome dismissal and replay do not change server-owned faction setup progress, flag delivery receipts, faction membership, claims, inventory, achievements or daily rewards. Start here and Help remain manually available regardless of the marker. Faction setup synchronization always runs independently of the marker; updated clients and servers must match the revised faction setup packet.

Successful faction creation, through either a command or its action form, starts optional guidance. The server opens the existing **Get a City Center** form, confirms delivery of the named flag, closes the form for world placement, then opens **Set Faction Home** only after accepted placement establishes a city. The small instruction panel stays at the top left during play; narrow action forms reserve space above their content. A server-confirmed valid faction home queues a short menu saying **Run /xmulti to get a multitool.** It explains the free Alloy Multitool's block harvesting and lack of durability. **Play** or Escape acknowledges this final guidance; the screen never runs the command or grants an item. Rejected commands do not trigger it, and another active screen delays opening. Repeated snapshots and later home changes do not reopen an acknowledged step for the same faction.

**Resume faction setup** and **Dismiss guidance / Show guidance** are available in Start here and the Help topic list. Closing a form keeps the confirmed step. Dismissal is saved and never prevents normal play; reopening resumes the saved step without repeating delivery. Existing faction membership alone does not start guidance. Failed commands keep the form and its arguments, with the server's reason; resolve that reason and retry. During placement, a rejection keeps the flag and the placement instruction. Check the server's chat message for foundation, territory, dimension or protection restrictions.

The initial delivery step and inventory are committed together in the existing UUID player snapshot. Repeated claims, reopened forms and reconnects cannot issue another setup flag while its placement is outstanding, including with survival recipes enabled. Find that flag in your inventory, storage or dropped items before trying to place it; the guide does not replace lost flags. Costs, rank checks, first-city capital designation and placement rules still come from the existing server handlers. No tutorial completion is required to play. Active visible setup and its completion message take priority over automatic daily-wheel openings; queued daily rewards remain available after completion or dismissal, and `/xdaily` still opens them manually.

Guide state is the optional `FactionSetup` compound (`Faction` UUID, confirmed `Step`, and `Hidden`) under `ForgeData.PlayerPersisted.xfDailyRewards`. It uses the existing player save/load/clone path and atomic player-file replacement. Client intents show, dismiss or resume guidance and acknowledge the final multitool information; they cannot set gameplay progress or grant items. Historical completed records keep `Step=4` and stay complete. Only newly confirmed homes enter `Step=5`; its acknowledgement saves the existing terminal `Step=4`. A disconnected pending step resumes on login rather than replaying claim/flag/home steps. Accepted city/home world changes retain Minecraft's existing world-save behavior; the player and world files are not one crash-atomic transaction. Updated clients and servers must match for the extended onboarding snapshot. In-game layout at different scales, cancellation, reconnect/restart, full inventories, storage failures and dedicated-server operation still require runtime verification.

## Native faction workers

Builder Contracts, Farmer Contracts (three variants) and Miner Contracts are available in the Xenofactions creative tab or through ordinary administrator item distribution. They are not granted by setup, `/xmulti`, crafting or city founding. Contract use requires an Officer/Leader on faction-owned land. A Builder Contract is used on a Builder Depot; farmer/miner contracts are used against a block face with two air blocks for the worker. Contracts are consumed only after a successful survival spawn. The native Forge entities require no NPC mod.

The **Builder** and its supporting Depot/job/schematic system were recovered from the local `e4cba6f` parent (the implementation introduced in `3d4eb18`), not from CustomNPCs or external configuration. The historical `entity_hfr_faction_builder` entity ID/name, `tileentity_hfr_builder`, `tile.builder`, `item.builder_contract`, faction/job/depot UUIDs and 27 carried inventory slots are retained. Unloading a worker does not authorize a replacement. Right-click the worker for its inventory/status and Recall/Pause/Resume; right-click the Depot for planning, materials and queue management. Depot slot 0 stays hidden legacy storage, with construction materials in slots 1–27. Place server `.schematic` files in `config/schematics`, select/position/rotate/mirror a plan, preview it, supply real materials, and start. Accepted jobs and normalized schematics use the historical `hfr_builder_jobs` and `hfr_schematic_store` world data. Optional Schematica client import remains optional; instant printing, removed machines and their dependencies are not restored. Schematics requiring missing blocks stop rather than granting substitutions. World/entity saves retain normal Minecraft durability.

The **Farmer** restores the historical `EntityFarmer` (`entity_hfr_slave`) and its three biped skins. Every five seconds of supplied-food time it works nearby mature crops at its own height, replants supported crops, moistens farmland and occasionally tills dirt/grass. The original above-Y=40/open-sky requirement, initial five-minute fuel, food nutrition × 1200-tick refill, yields and crop specialization are retained: O'Brien favors potatoes, Nghiêm also harvests upper sugar cane and favors rice, and Davis also harvests pumpkins/melons. Right-click with food to feed; otherwise right-click to collect its stored produce. A full player inventory leaves the remainder on the farmer. Operations and collection are server-owned and limited to its faction; legacy saved farmers without a faction stay idle until an Officer/Leader binds them by interacting on that faction's land. Saved crop counts, fuel and type are retained. No new crop or world generation is added.

The **Miner** uses an opaque, normally lit vanilla biped and displays its actual equipped pickaxe. First authorized interaction supplies two UUID/dimension-bound wands. The receipt survives reconnect/death; **Recover / bind wands** reuses existing wands for this Miner and replaces missing ones. Both grants are checked for free inventory space before changing anything; full inventories retain the previous binding and can retry. Wands identify the Miner and controls in their tooltips. A bound Miner must remain loaded in the same dimension, but the player need not stand beside it while selecting. After a reload, lookup examines at most 4096 loaded entities if its cached entity ID changed; if it cannot resolve the Miner, use Recover / bind wands beside it.

Right-click a vanilla chest with the **Miner Station Wand** to assign supply/output storage without opening it. Both halves of a double chest are used and must be loaded and on faction land; blocked chest lids prevent access. Faction Builder Depots remain supported, excluding their legacy hidden slot. Station assignment pauses the worker and shows coordinates/dimension in its GUI. Use the **Mining Area Wand**: left-click corner A, right-click corner B for an inclusive 3D box, or **Shift-right-click B** for a horizontal footprint extending from the higher of the two selected Y values down through **Y:0**. This uses the configured Sneak control's state at the click, including rebound mouse/keyboard controls; releasing Sneak before the server handles the packet cannot change that intent. Changing A retains the last B depth mode; a later ordinary right-click B intentionally returns to corner-height mode. Native interactions only suppress breaking/placing/opening and cannot overwrite the custom packet's selection. Reversed corners normalize automatically. Server feedback, tooltips and the GUI show the explicit depth mode, effective top/bottom Y and inclusive volume. The held wand previews the boundary and selected blocks.

Return to the Miner and press **Start / Resume**. Start resolves this Miner's UUID/dimension-bound area wand on the server, validates both raw corners, normalized bounds, station and faction permissions, and assigns and starts a first job in one action. Server-issued, persisted selection revisions prefer the most recently edited bound wand over older copies; conflicting selections with equal revisions (including old unversioned wands) remain ambiguous and are rejected. Incomplete, invalid, wrong-dimension and foreign-only selections also explain their rejection. Selecting the same area resumes without resetting its cursor or current running mining progress. A different area replacing unfinished work prompts once with **Replace & Start**. The server container retains the exact proposal, including depth mode, and the GUI shows those proposed bounds separately. Confirmation revalidates that stored proposal, the assigned job, station and permissions, then installs and starts it immediately, even if the wand subsequently moves, changes or is removed. To replace the proposal with a later selection, cancel with Pause and press Start again. Selecting alone leaves the old job unchanged; a failed Start preserves it and never silently resumes it instead. With no new selection, Start resumes the saved job without either wand. Installed bounds and mode are saved independently, and activation never clears wand selections.

The GUI exposes all existing 27 cargo slots, the actual equipped pickaxe with durability, a dedicated torch supply slot, and player inventory/hotbar with normal and Shift-click transfers. The right panel shows loading or a synchronization warning while awaiting server state, then started/paused state, the specific work or blocking reason, installed station and bounds before requests, task/target and the separate wand selection. Use its up/down buttons or mouse wheel to read wrapped details. Activation errors persist through polling; a successful action clears obsolete feedback. An unapplied selection or replacement proposal is not an installed job. Replacement confirmation belongs to the current server container and is discarded when it closes; changing the installed assignment requires fresh confirmation. The Miner sends its authoritative UUID in Forge spawn data: 1.7.10 otherwise creates a different client UUID and the required server UUID checks reject both Start and status requests. Actions and replies validate the current container window; replies retain connection/world/entity checks and a per-window sequence rejects older snapshots. Selection updates synchronize item NBT and any open Miner panel, activation replies carry the installed server state, and open panels also refresh once per second. Matching updated clients and servers are required for the expanded click packet.

Tools and torches can be supplied directly or through the station; resources can be retrieved. Blocking requests check equipment, cargo and the accessible assigned station. A missing usable pickaxe requests one suitable pickaxe; an insufficient tool shows the required harvest level. A dark work area with no available torches requests one torch to unblock lighting. The optional refill to 32 is displayed separately and never blocks work by itself. Missing/unloaded/inaccessible storage is an access problem, not proof of a supply shortage. An active job privately notifies its online employer of confirmed shortages, identifies the Miner and explains both supply locations; unchanged requests are deduplicated between reminders, with at most one reminder per Miner per 60 seconds, even across repeated Start clicks or reopening the GUI. A new ladder shortage episode sends its first notice promptly, then uses the same 60-second reminder interval. Recipient faction permissions are rechecked. No open GUI is required. Fulfilled requests, completion and manual Pause stop reminders.

The worker fetches suitable replacements, retains metadata/NBT and partial transfer remainders, and rechecks a supply wait within 2.5 seconds (direct inventory changes wake it sooner). It resumes with the same cursor and retained alternative target after restocking; directly supplied required tools/torches can cancel a supply trip while usable cargo capacity remains, including a partially loaded trip. Start / Resume and opening the GUI never create a deposit merely because cargo is present. Inventory slot changes no longer erase mining progress; changing the actual tool or target still resets it, and repeated Start while already running preserves progress. It places supported torches near itself when local combined light is below 8, respecting faction ownership and Forge placement cancellation, and consumes supplies only after accepted placement. A blocked lighting position has its own status. Loaded workers remain paused until Start.

The worker still walks to storage and mines real blocks top-down through Forge survival harvesting with a FakePlayer, native hardness and durability. Source tracing confirms this raises BreakEvent into the existing loaded custom stone-drop table. For Miner harvests only, the existing reward handler is deferred until the actual harvest succeeds and the event remains uncanceled; it rolls once, at the mined block's Y, preserving configured chances, quantities, metadata, NBT and Y limits alongside ordinary drops. Cargo collects the resulting real item entities and delivers them to storage. There is no separate Miner loot table or offline production.

Mining requires faction-owned land and continued assigning-member authority. Containers, tile entities, faction infrastructure and liquids remain protected; bedrock/negative-hardness blocks are skipped. Limits remain 64×64 horizontally, world-height depth including Y:0, 262144 total blocks and 128 horizontal blocks from storage to every area corner. Work remains top-down; alternative current-layer targets retain unfinished primary work. Navigation uses collision shapes and the Miner's body for walking, steps up of at most one block, safe drops of at most two blocks with independently verified exits, and attached ladders. Partial surfaces such as slabs and stairs use their actual collision height. Ladder nodes need attachment and clearance, not ordinary floor support or onGround. All height-changing and partial-surface segments run through ordinary living movement/collision physics, using the same one-block step height as planning; vanilla receives at most six straight level edges. Accepted ladder edges use separate vertical travel, lip clearance, horizontal exit and floor landing phases. Vertical impulses are capped to the remaining distance; safely crossed rung boundaries advance without reversing back to the rung. Horizontal exit and landing keep normal gravity. An idle working Miner holds its position on the ladder. Hypothetical rungs use their proposed vanilla collision shape, metadata and attachment, and proposed backing also blocks interaction sight lines. Actual construction/travel still checks blocks already present.

Candidate endpoints and actual arrival share reach, body clearance, footing or ladder attachment, elevation, visibility and target-identity checks. The baseline allowed a center exactly three blocks away while native navigation could finish within 0.6 blocks of that center, leaving the real Miner outside reach. Route ranking now adds a preference for endpoints within 2.35 blocks, leaving movement tolerance inside unchanged three-block reach. Valid fringe endpoints remain available. A completed route with a small reach/visibility/footing disagreement can attempt one short, supported, collision-checked final approach. It must pass actual arrival again before interacting. Failed endpoints are excluded for that target, and changed blocks or metadata invalidate travel. Logs identify the actual failed condition, target identity, actual position, selected endpoint, distance, ground/ladder state and visibility. The localized GUI separates the target, selected standing position, first planned descent, current waypoint, actual position and walking-rim/stepping/descending/climbing/construction/supply states.

A* compares complete route costs across interaction positions above and below the old surface and searches the whole bounded corridor for direct steps and existing rim entrances before constructing access. Each search expands at most 8192 positions, discovers at most 16384 and retains at most 4096 route positions; per-search surface caching is capped at 32768 entries. Work-route selection checks at most three endpoints per update, retaining the remaining endpoints across updates for independent storage-exit checks. The exit may differ from the entrance and is checked with the target absent before descent and again before harvesting; entrance travel still uses the actual unmined terrain. Travel stays inside the footprint plus eight blocks or the existing sixteen-block station/drop corridors. Paths and nearby collision shapes are revalidated, searches use loaded blocks only, and native follower-neighbor chunks are checked before submission and each tick. A hundred ticks without route progress triggers replanning, capped at four rebuilds or three failed route attempts. Failed endpoints are bounded to 64 per target. Terminal failures preserve the job/items and use the existing one-minute logger limit. A search limit does not establish that every possible entrance is obstructed.

If a started Miner is displaced outside that corridor, or **Start / Resume** is used on an existing displaced Miner, it first shows **Returning to work corridor**. The previous combined outside/unsupported-start rejection happened before any route expansion; it did not establish a terrain obstruction. Recovery now searches for any reachable supported corridor entry instead of assuming the nearest geometric entry is accessible. Its fixed envelope is centered on the initial displacement position: a 64-block horizontal radius and at most 16 blocks vertically, including all candidate entries within that envelope. Each search retains the 8192 expanded / 16384 discovered node limits and a route cost of at most 256 level steps (height changes cost extra). At most three searches, including replans, and 2400 recovery ticks are allowed. Movement never recenters or enlarges these bounds. Recovery checks loaded terrain, support, body collision shapes, dry clearance and reversible one-block steps, and follows the same short verified native segments as ordinary travel. Its route is separate from work travel and the storage return cache. The GUI shows the recovery start, selected reachable destination (or that no entry has been selected), attempts and localized outside-corridor, unsupported-footing, obstruction, unloaded-chunk or search-limit reasons. Terminal failures use the existing once-per-Miner-per-minute logger and retain the job and items.

Recovery temporarily owns movement before work scans or inventory-driven storage decisions. Reaching a safe corridor position clears its temporary path and resumes the interrupted mining, drop collection or storage task on the next work tick, retaining the assigned area, cursor, pending drops, cargo, equipment, supplies and trip reason; partial cargo alone still does not trigger a deposit. The original job corridor remains authoritative for all normal routes. Pause cancels recovery and requires Start again. Reload retains the interrupted task and existing job/items, remains paused as before, and rebuilds recovery from loaded terrain on Start; transient recovery paths are never restored from disk. A bounded failure requires inspection of its actual reason, not respawning or reassigning the Miner. Recovery still needs in-game validation against the actual terrain, including displaced saved jobs, blocked nearest entries, chunk unloading, collisions and interrupted storage trips.

Multi-layer assignments retain original terrain along the existing perimeter ramp, with one block of rise per step. When ordinary walking cannot provide both work access and a safe return, the same access planner can preserve and complete this ramp as a **staircase made from full support blocks**. No crafted stair items are needed. The planner reuses suitable terrain, fills missing treads and their short supporting foundations, and reserves the Miner's body and lift clearance. Each successive tread must pass reversible one-block movement and body collision checks. The bottom must connect to the current exposed floor, and the upper landing must connect to the station route. A narrow straight footprint can support a staircase only where its available length and clearance actually permit those connections. A single column, obstructed headroom outside the assignment, or insufficient owned room can prevent construction; the Miner does not expand the selection.

Existing usable routes take priority. The Miner also discovers player-placed ladders in the assigned excavation, in bounded column scans, even when no supplied rungs are available. It evaluates complete existing ladders, then repairs/extensions of existing columns, then eligible new ladder layouts before generating any staircase proposals. Distance/material cost ranks alternatives within those priorities; it cannot put a staircase ahead of an eligible ladder route. Access validation checks attachment, a reversible connection from the exposed floor, an existing upper landing and storage access. Local mining separately selects reachable interaction positions and checks their post-harvest exit; an inaccessible individual target cannot replace a usable fixed ladder with a staircase. Failed connections report their coordinates and condition; a failed route search does not establish that no ladder exists. Ladder material eligibility counts missing rungs and backing against cargo plus physically reachable station supplies. An existing rung whose backing needs repair does not require another ladder item. A route or attachment prerequisite temporarily missing from a committed ladder is repair work, not a reason to choose a perimeter staircase. The retained repair waits for executable local operations or genuine missing supplies; ordinary staircase proposals remain available when no committed ladder can be recovered. A shortage in a committed ladder route instead starts the explicit escape/restocking task before more excavation or descent.

A verified existing ladder fixes the selection's X/Z column and attachment face; otherwise the **first successfully placed ladder** fixes it. Existing attachment outside the selection may be reused, while new backing and rungs must remain inside the assigned bounds and owned land. Extensions and repairs use the retained column and landing/backing reservations. Repairable terrain in the fixed column, ladder entry, headroom or connecting landing becomes an authorized clearance operation; reservations still exclude those cells from ordinary excavation. Protected blocks, containers, liquids, bedrock and unbreakable blocks remain forbidden. The fixed column and face survive AOE damage. Recorded placed access and saved ladder positions remain compatible. Target changes, Pause, Start, storage trips and reload retain the column; installing a replacement mining selection alone clears it. Delivering ladders during ordinary staircase construction triggers one reconsideration at a supported edit boundary; it never interrupts an active escape climb merely to reconsider ordinary access layouts. A retained fixed ladder replaces the unfinished staircase objective as soon as repair can be planned, without demanding that its broken exit already work. Already placed staircase blocks remain in the world and recorded as history/reservations; they are not automatically rebuilt or extended by ladder repair. Jobs containing both access types reconcile their fixed ladder from the actual saved position without changing the selection or Miner. A saved running job resumes automatically; a manually paused job stays paused. Uncommitted ladder alternatives still leave the staircase order and index intact when no ladder can be adopted. Unchanged supplies do not repeatedly switch plans.

The fixed ladder has a separate saved requirement set (`MinerLadderAccess`) for its column/face, backing, entry connection and upper landing. `MinerAccess` remains the history of accepted edits, including obsolete staircase cells; copying that history into both repair startup and reconciliation previously made staircase treads ladder repair requirements. Mixed old saves recover fixed rungs and their backing, identify a station-connected upper landing and rediscover the entry from supported terrain near the retained unfinished target. Lower extension uses that retained work position, separately from recorded damage; a station trip or repair position cannot move the underground floor to the surface. Only an actual supported position with usable body clearance before and after target removal can extend the column, never hypothetical deeper terrain. One executable plan retains those active requirements through approach, construction, supply waits and interrupted storage trips. Dependencies are local: clear the next reachable obstruction/body space, inspect the actual AOE result, restore backing, then attach the dependent rung and climb to the next safe working position. Unreachable upper clearance does not gate lower repairs. Reachable higher clearance is tried before restoring its lower stage to reduce repeated AOE damage. A completed clearance prerequisite stays satisfied when its replacement is installed, preventing a clear/replace loop. The fixed shaft, floor entry, upper exit and body/head clearance are reserved before attachment selection. Permanent clearance and rungs take precedence over support requirements; temporary clearance before a replacement stays only in the operation plan. Missing backing attaches through a shortest face-connected support chain to actual authorized terrain, bounded to five cells from the requested support. Its anchor must survive completion, and the completed chain must preserve the floor/ladder/station route; each block must also have a real attachment at execution. Hypothetical supports cannot authorize actual placement. Staircase construction retains its ordered steps. Both try alternative reachable builder positions and eligible pending operations when an edit fails. It does not discard the whole objective after one local failure. The Miner can build a safe first step while the rest remains unfinished, and can spend the suitable blocks it already carries before waiting for more. Hypothetical edits exist only in the route overlay. Before committing an edit, the Miner checks its actual body position, collision clearance and support. It never places a block inside its body. Negative clearance operations use the same authorized Forge survival harvesting and successful, uncanceled custom stone-drop path as normal mining; actual output is collected or saved in the existing overflow buffer. Clearance and support execution are separate, and the construction index advances only after success or an already satisfied operation. Planned clearance targets and placements stay inside assigned bounds and owned land. Clearance uses the supplied pickaxe with its normal AOE geometry and per-block Forge harvesting; secondary clearance breaks outside the owned assignment are rejected at the event boundary. Every break respects ownership, protection events, protected blocks, tile entities, liquids, bedrock and unbreakable blocks. The planner will not select headroom outside the top bound. Planning verifies a complete route separately from executing each edit: post-edit reachability must preserve an existing station exit, or reach storage/the retained supported safe area for a trapped Miner. Fixed-ladder repair refreshes that refuge from the actual supported working area after climbing/AOE, so a lost lower refuge cannot require the entire exit to be repaired before the next local edit. Tool-driven terrain changes invalidate that proof and require routes and affected construction to be checked again through the existing repair/escape behavior. The return search may take another path; it does not require reversing the original approach nodes. Native placement checks, edit permission, attachment, entity collision, Forge PlaceEvent and rollback remain authoritative; only accepted placement consumes a real supplied item.

Supply plain untagged solid stone/earth ItemBlocks such as cobblestone or dirt; falling blocks, tile-entity blocks and tagged supplies remain excluded. Station materials count only after an actual station route is verified. If it is unreachable, carried blocks establish access and shortages request the exact remaining support block count for direct delivery. An ordinary staircase requests no ladders; an escape caused by a ladder shortage retains its separate ladder restocking request. Material delivery or relevant terrain changes automatically resume the retained plan; unchanged blocked conditions do not restart the same search. The Miner rechecks station reachability after construction changes the terrain. Required restocking retains reserved construction supplies in cargo, deposits other output and returns automatically to the selected access plan, then unfinished mining. Storage trips and work returns use the same collision-checked access geometry.

Only missing required backing/rungs or a newly exposed floor create access supply needs. Spare rungs and emergency stone/earth reserves never initiate a trip or prevent ordinary floor movement. Useful carried reserves are retained during deposits and may refill during an already necessary station visit, without waiting for optional amounts. When actual missing requirements exceed carried supplies, **Building / following escape route** becomes the saved task and **Ladder restocking** becomes the storage reason. The target, cursor, assignment and fixed ladder position stay intact. Existing safe station routes take priority. Otherwise the Miner searches nearby reachable floors (within four columns in each horizontal direction, including safe drops), and plans a pillar of at most sixteen blocks with a real upper landing connected to the station. This escape does not require a reversible connection to a distant first staircase tread or to its original floor. Each rise uses a normal entity jump, checks the actual body before placing beneath its feet through the existing placement/protection/rollback path, spends one real carried item only on success, and lands before repeating. Only necessary authorized headroom is harvested. Missing blocks or obstruction retain the plan, coordinates and detailed failure; the Miner does not claim station arrival. Relevant deliveries/terrain changes wake it. Direct ladder delivery to a blocked, supported Miner can resume fixed-route repair instead of requiring more escape blocks.

At the station it deposits ordinary output while keeping emergency/construction supplies, takes the required ladders, and repairs/rechecks the fixed column and exposed-floor connection before resuming excavation. Ladder delivery invalidates stale route failures. A short connecting landing, straight or with a turn over a span of up to five blocks, can bridge the work floor to the ladder bottom. Its plan includes entry footing and necessary body/head clearance as well as missing supports. The completed connection is checked in the planning overlay; actual clearance and placement each require a reachable builder position with safe post-edit footing and access to storage or the retained safe area. Repair does not require the broken completed connection to exist before edits can begin. If the station lacks ladders, the Miner waits there and privately tells its still-authorized employer the Miner identity, station coordinates and missing count. The first notice is prompt and repeated reminders are limited to one per minute. Restocking clears the shortage; manual Pause stops actions and reminders. Escape origin, selected pillar, landing, pending edits, required block/ladder restocking quantities and repair intent survive reload. Running workers resume from their actual saved position; manual Pause still requires Start.

The Miner accepts pickaxes through `ItemPickaxe` inheritance or Forge's `pickaxe` tool class, with remaining durability, harvest level and the target block's harvest rules determining suitability. Supplied tool modes, abilities, enchantments, durability and NBT are left to the tool's normal harvest hooks. Excavation and clearance share Forge survival harvesting, including AOE secondary breaks, without a primary-coordinate restriction or changes to normal player harvesting. The scope retains all break attempts and their final cancellation state. Custom stone rewards use each removed stone block's own coordinates and Y range, once per affected cell; canceled events, unchanged blocks and metadata-only changes earn no rewards. Secondary removal is checked independently of the primary harvest result. Reentrant callbacks share their actor's action, and scoped state is cleared after completion or an exception. Real item entities from all changed sites are collected through the existing cargo/overflow path, including partial actions; overlapping pickup volumes cannot collect an entity twice. The cursor advances only when its own target is cleared, and later scans recognize other cells already cleared by AOE. Changed terrain invalidates travel and exit proofs. The Miner compares retained backing, rungs, entry footing, landings and clearance with actual terrain, including ladder drops caused by neighbor updates without a BreakEvent. Missing backing precedes dependent rungs, and already satisfied operations are skipped. Only required access is restored, using real carried materials and the existing placement events/rollback. Real detached ladder drops join ordinary cargo/overflow collection. After harvesting, canonical access requirements are inspected for actual missing, obstructed or invalid cells, including detached rungs; an unchanged route does not request repair after each pass. A carried-supply repair starts immediately and retains the work target and unfinished operations. Completion checks actual geometry and floor/ladder/station route availability without issuing movement commands. There are no bottom/top/upper-landing verification trips. Local movement to perform a necessary edit remains allowed. Actual traversal is checked by the ordinary movement path during legitimate work, repair or required station travel. Completed repairs resume unfinished work locally, or resume an interrupted station trip only while its recorded need remains. Access policy 3 saves the work-floor position and its target separately from movement destinations. Reload retains the selection, depth, cursor, station, inventory, fixed column and running/paused intent; older saves discard obsolete pending planning/verification objectives and rebuild the fixed connection using actual terrain near the saved target. Missing or stale work-floor fields are rediscovered automatically. Reload also reconciles conflicting passage/support requirements and rebuilds routes from the actual position. Missing, broken or insufficient-tier tools still trigger ordinary pickaxe supply requests.

The GUI retains all coordinates, ladder position, proposal/endpoint/stage counters, material quantities, detailed failures and scrolling controls, and adds the escape landing/pillar, emergency reserve, ladder counts and restocking/repair intent. It shows **Building / following escape route**, **Jumping to place escape support**, **At station; waiting for ladders**, **Using ladder**, **Extending ladder**, **Repairing ladder**, **Clearing ladder entry / headroom**, **Replacing ladder backing**, **Replacing ladder rungs**, **Repairing ladder landing / footing** or **Building staircase** for the actual activity, with exact material shortages and the useful blocker before planner counters. Blocked construction shows its blocking state. Proposal, endpoint and stage counters remain in detailed diagnostics, alongside the exact current clearance/backing/rung operation. Cargo diagnostics show empty slots, partial-stack capacity and any actual overflow item's compatible remaining capacity. Missing attachment, missing materials, unreachable clearance, native placement refusal and an observed canceled Forge PlaceEvent have separate diagnostic messages; the last actual placement failure remains visible while alternate builder endpoints are tried. An attachment failure before placement is not described as event cancellation. Pending construction order/index, staircase alignment, placed supports, clearance reservations, ladder position, construction safe area and interrupted storage intent are saved with the job. Older jobs without these fields keep their installed bounds, depth, cursor, station, inventory and pending drops; routes rebuild on Start. Manual Pause stays stopped until Start; saved running jobs resume automatically after reload. Deferred cleanup remembers the original terrain ramp alignment even when supplied access selects a different alignment. Active staircase supports, headroom, ladders and backing remain excluded from primary excavation targets during ordinary work and deferred ramp cleanup. Supplied AOE tools retain their normal geometry; affected access is revalidated and repaired when needed. An actively selected staircase can extend its ramp as excavation exposes deeper layers; fixed ladder repair does not extend obsolete staircase history. Retained access NBT accepts up to 4096 support/clearance records; the pending plan reader accepts up to 8192 operations so a clearance prerequisite cannot truncate its dependent replacement on reload. Route search limits are unchanged. Actual construction, protection interactions, storage return and integrated/dedicated-server traversal remain unverified in game.

Route searches join nearby supported nodes from the Miner's actual body position, with collision-checked initial motion executed through normal physics. A grounded worker straddling a block edge is no longer rejected solely because the center of its floored coordinate lacks support. Actual and hypothetical return searches and cached routes use the same initial-connection check. The selected mining position must keep the same feet height before and after removal; if the target is its footing, another position is selected. Both the per-tick arrival check and work updates finish the approved endpoint rather than canceling descent as soon as the target enters reach. Alternative current-layer blocks are selected only after verifying an approach, interaction position and post-harvest storage exit, or selecting a complete supplied-access plan. While evaluating an alternative, a retained access proposal resumes its own pending endpoint checks on the next update; its pending flag does not prevent calling the planner again. Fixed-ladder travel joins the upper landing, attached column, exposed work floor and a valid mining endpoint through the existing collision, footing and landing rules. A verified dry exit may skip the remaining lower rungs when the existing two-block drop limit permits it; actual feet height is checked before leaving. Exit validation joins the selected position back to the column and station with the target removed; it does not change the live work destination. Short entry repairs are planned from the retained floor and real reachable terrain anchors before mining. A failed candidate does not reject the other endpoints or replace the committed ladder with a staircase. Endpoint failures are scoped to the exact proposed geometry and actual search origin, invalidated by relevant observed terrain changes, and never prune another hypothetical component. Search bounds remain separate from physical obstruction. Failed blocks remain unfinished and are reconsidered after excavation, construction or a detected relevant change. Searching and access planning are separate activities; changing targets or rebuilding the same route is not mining progress.

Precise movement retains the existing 0.01-block horizontal tolerance, 0.01-block floor-height tolerance and 0.06-block rung-height tolerance. Vertical travel keeps the planned edge direction through temporary ground/ladder flag changes. Completion also consumes a safely crossed boundary within one rung of the goal, with the body centered inside the clear, attached column; it never reverses toward a passed rung. Before a floor exit, actual feet height must satisfy the unchanged drop bound. An upward exit first clears the lip, then horizontal exit and landing retain normal gravity and collision resolution. Floor completion requires the approved endpoint and a grounded landing; it does not require centering on a rung while leaving it. Every precise phase measures decreasing distance to completion, including zero vertical commands and stalled landing. Twenty movement ticks without improvement reject the active edge and release its commands. Replanning starts from the actual body in the existing work update, with failed directed edges excluded until observed terrain changes, accepted construction/harvesting or an explicit objective retry. Both precise and native walking edges advance during movement updates; handoffs clear stale navigation, movement-helper, jump and precise commands. A valid native walking command is not frozen by a lingering ladder flag. Interaction, sight, footing and mining reach checks remain unchanged. Routes retain the target and task; no verification ascent, station trip or staircase is introduced to repair controller failure. Saved running jobs rebuild movement from their actual position without resetting their job; manual Pause remains paused. All GUI diagnostics remain, with active phase, route indices, precise goal, no-progress ticks and the last segment failure added. Existing ladder warnings retain their ten-second per-Miner limit and requested/actual vertical and native-velocity values, adding phase and requested/actual horizontal displacement. These are source-validated controller behavior, not an observed in-game traversal result.

Ordinary route retries use `xf.miner.state.recovering` ("Recovering a bounded route to the current task"); displaced-corridor recovery uses `xf.miner.state.corridor_recovery`. The old fallback's `xf.miner.state.working` ("Working") did not prove that a route was executable and is no longer emitted by that selection path. `xf.miner.nav.footing` identifies missing safe footing/attached ladder; `xf.miner.nav.access_blocked` and `xf.miner.nav.access_limit` describe unsuccessful or bounded access planning. Route search/terrain failure (`xf.miner.nav.path`) is separate from stalled segment execution (`xf.miner.nav.stalled`), whose retained details also identify native walking rejection; construction exit validation and direct-supply waits have separate blockers. The GUI puts activity, evaluated target and blocker first, keeps proposal/endpoint progress and the failed access stage with coordinates in its detailed diagnostics, retains final failure details while blocked and clears resolved arrival errors. Rate-limited access diagnostics include occupied ladder cells, missing attachment, floor/upper-landing connections, work approach/exit, first placement, incomplete full construction exits and preservation of existing escape/reachable area. A failed full search retains its proposal, phase, start, destination and one representative rejected frontier movement with coordinates and rule; it does not log every rejected neighbor. The GUI distinguishes location scanning, work approach and the storage exit from an already reachable work endpoint, displays the retained work-floor coordinates separately, and identifies the failed ladder/landing/floor/station segment with the rejected movement coordinates and rule. Unfinished checks remain an access-planning activity.

The unfinished job retains a separate limit of 24 failed execution attempts and 12000 active work ticks without useful progress across task transitions and alternate targets. Actual hardness progress, completed cursor advancement and first-time reached route positions advance the progress clock; harvests, accepted access placement and real storage transfers also clear the failure budget. Reaching a new route position can clear execution failures, but revisiting the same positions on a rebuilt route cannot. Supply/chunk/storage waits do not consume the active clock. Exhausted alternatives or bounds enter a stable started-but-blocked state instead of rerunning the same task. The Miner inspects up to 512 of at most 32768 recorded planner terrain cells per update (block identity, metadata and loadedness), relevant supply/station availability and its position. Detected changes automatically retry retained work or construction; unchanged conditions preserve the final blocker. Silent collision changes within a tile entity that do not change block identity/metadata are not detected by this terrain monitor. Start remains an explicit manual retry, and manual Pause remains stopped until Start. Reload restores the saved running/paused state. Objective changes, execution failures, access attempts and failure resets have separate ten-second diagnostic limits; terminal diagnostics retain the existing one-minute limit. The explicit work cycle is travel to unfinished work, mining, travel to storage with a recorded reason, depositing, required restocking, then automatic travel back to unfinished work. Trips begin only for cargo with no remaining stack capacity, actual output that cannot fit compatible item/metadata/NBT stacks, a required pickaxe, required dark-area torches, required access materials, or the final deposit. After deposit and required restocking, the storage destination, waypoint and retry state are replaced by the retained work/drop target without another Start or area reset. Overflow keeps copies of the actual uncollected item stacks in saved Miner NBT and removes those source item entities exactly once. ## 1. Join or create a faction

Create a faction:

```text
/c create <faction name>
```

Join an existing faction:

```text
/c apply <faction name>
```

Faction officers/leaders can review and accept applicants:

```text
/c applicants
/c accept <player>
/c deny <player>
```

Officers and leaders can also send `/c invite <player>` while the target is online. The target accepts with `/c join <faction name>`. Revoke an invitation with `/c uninvite <player>`, including while the target is offline. Invitations survive faction renames and server restarts, and joining a faction clears all pending invitations.

## 2. Claim your first city

Stand where you want the city center and run:

```text
/c claim <city name>
```

City rules are configurable by the server. By default, city radii are tiered from 2 to 6 chunks, city centers must be spaced apart, and city upgrades cost prestige.

Place the named City Center to found the city. Your first successful city becomes the faction's designated capital, separately from its upgrade level. Officers and leaders can run `/c sethome` only inside that capital. Inspect it with `/c capital`; leaders can change it with `/c capital set <owned city>`, with seven days between changes by default.

When survival recipes are enabled, craft a Global Market Terminal using `IRI / ECE / IRI` (I iron ingot, R redstone, E emerald, C chest). An Officer or leader must place it in your capital. Each faction gets one active terminal, and all members can browse the server's approved shops there. If the capital changes, place a replacement there; the old terminal stays inactive. Breaking the active terminal lets your faction place another.

Upgrade a city while standing in it:

```text
/c city upgrade
```

Name a claim:

```text
/c nameclaim <claim name>
```

## 3. Earn and spend prestige

Prestige is the faction economy. It is used for upkeep, war, warps, and city growth.

Useful commands:

```text
/c balance
/c deposit <amount>
/c setwarp <name>
/c warp <name>
/c warps
```

Withdrawal is unavailable. Old Prestige tokens have no acquisition path; depositing existing tokens remains a compatibility path into faction accounting.

## 4. Manage faction identity

Leaders can set presentation and messaging:

```text
/c color <hexadecimal>
/c motd <message>
/c flag <flag>
/c flag seturl <https image URL>
/c flag clear
/c flag reload
```

Use `/c listflags` to browse built-in flags. Imported image flags must pass the server's custom-flag configuration.

## 5. Diplomacy and war

Common diplomacy commands:

```text
/c ally <faction>
/c acceptally <faction>
/c unally <faction>
/c declarewar <faction>
/c ceasefire <faction>
/c acceptceasefire <faction>
/c peace <faction> {city}
/c acceptpeace <faction>
/c surrender <faction>
/c acceptsurrender <faction>
/c defendally <ally>
```

War declarations may be globally disabled by admins, and many war actions have configurable cooldowns and online-player checks.

## 6. Chat and maps

Faction chat:

```text
/cc <message>
/cc faction <message>
/cc alliance <message>
/cc public
```

Claim map item:

```text
/xmap
```

## Tips

- Use `/c help 1` through `/c help 6` in game for command pages.
- Faction names with spaces are stored with underscores in some command paths; when a command fails with a spaced name, try underscores.
- Ask server staff before using war, surrender, city transfer, or custom image features; many are tuned by server policy.
